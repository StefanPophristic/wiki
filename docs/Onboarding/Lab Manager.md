### A quick note about the guides

If you have a question about an administrative task, your first step should be to take a look at a guide. Other lab members are a great resource -- our lab is full of nice, knowledgeable people. ### Slack

Most lab communication happens via Slack (our workspace is called `nyu-nellab`). This includes lab-wide announcements about lab meetings, lab outings, talks, etc. Staff receive "Workspace Admin" status, allowing them to add new members.

### Petty Cash

The NeLLab has a petty cash fund that is used to provide cash payments to study participants on both Alec & Liina sides of the lab. The lab manager is the petty cash custodian, which means that you will be in charge of maintaining the petty cash fund.

The cash lives in a metal case, on the top shelf of a marked safe on the 5th floor of Meyer. To open the safe and access the cash, you need:

1. Card access to the room containing the safes (Roman should take care of this when you take over the role of petty cash custodian)

2. The combination to the safe. You will have learned this from your predecessor; I'm not going to put it in writing.

We currently have a petty cash limit of $1200, which means that the [total amount of money listed on the receipts] + [the cash in the safe] at any given time should always equal $1200. To ensure that this is the case (i.e. no cash has been lost or stolen), the petty cash custodian meets with Roman Zamishka (`zamishka@nyu.edu`) once per month for petty cash reconciliation. If you're short on cash, there are missing receipts. If there's too much cash, someone has paid a participant out of pocket and hasn't been reimbursed. Make sure to train every new lab member about how petty cash works and how to fill out a payment receipt. You can find my instructions and blank receipts here:

### Petty Cash Reconciliation

Petty cash reconciliation is very simple. About 15 minutes before your appointment with Roman, collect all the receipts in the MEG lab (make sure you get Alec’s _and_ Liina’s). Then, collect all the cash from our safe. Count it all up to make sure the total is correct, and take note of how much of the fund is in cash vs. in receipts. Assuming the total is correct, take these items to Roman, let him know how much you have in cash vs. in receipts, and then sign the form he gives you after he counts it all up. Then, put everything back where you found it.

If the total is _not_ correct and we are missing cash:

1. Send a message on slack asking if anyone has any receipts held out of the folder they're supposed to be in. 

2. Ask if anyone has paid participants out-of-pocket and hasn't been reimbursed yet.
 
3. Worst case scenario, i.e. you can't find the missing cash and nobody says they have extra receipts or are owed cash, just let Roman know. Usually he will let it slide as long as you make sure it turns up for the next reconciliation.

### Paying Subjects

As I mentioned before, the petty cash is exclusively used for paying subjects (99% of the time, MEG subjects, but can be used for behavioral experiments as well). We pay at a rate of $15 per hour of time in the MEG lab, though in special cases (e.g. if a very specific population is required for a study), we can reimburse participants for travel as well. Note that, per the IRB, we must pay subjects for their time even if they do not complete the experiment. For instance, if someone shows up, but turns out to have metal in their mouth they forgot about, you would pay them $5 or so. Just like regular subject payments, this type of payment *always* requires a receipt.

Since you are the petty cash custodian, you will have partial responsibility for making sure lab members have cash to pay their subjects (as no one in the lab except you will have safe access). Double-check with them the amount they’ll need, get it from the safe, and give it to them before they run their participant(s).  It’s best to give them the amount in smaller bills if possible, in case of situations like the ones described above. If you aren't available to get petty cash, Jeff does have access to our fund and you can ask him to get cash out for people. 

If for some reason you AND Jeff are unable to get petty cash, the experimenter can front the cash themselves and you can reimburse them from the fund later. Note that they still need to fill out a receipt like usual.

### Petty Cash Refill

Depending on how many lab members are running studies and the types of studies that they’re running, the petty cash fund will be depleted at variable rates. For this reason, it’s a good idea to stay aware of how quickly the lab is using petty cash, as it is your job to refill the cash when it gets low.

 To begin the process, email Roman telling him you need a refill, or if this coincides with a reconciliation he'll probably just assume you need one if you're running low. Roman will take care of the paperwork there, and make a packet of receipts for you to bring to Student Services for a refill. 
 
Next, take the forms and receipts to the Student Services building on Lafayette. You will have to sign in on one of the tablets and select that you are there for petty cash purposes. When you are given a booth number, you can give the forms and receipts to the cashier at that booth. They will get you to sign them and then they’ll count out the required petty cash for you.  You can give them a specific breakdown of the bills you’d like, or just let them know how much you need in fives, tens, and twenties. Since the vast majority of our participants are paid $30 for a 2-hour booking, a breakdown could look like ~1110 in tens and twenties (37 x $10 and 37 x $20, good for 37 participants), and the rest in tens and fives for the occasional MRI subject (6 x $5 and 6 x $10). The cashier will keep the forms and receipts, and you will take the petty cash and put it in our box in the safe. Done!

Note that if the lab is going through petty cash particularly fast, you may need to start a new reconciliation process *before* the last one has been finished.

Occasionally, you may not be able to refill the cash fast enough and the fund will run out. This is not the end of the world, but it’s not good. If it runs out, you should make sure you start a petty cash refill *immediately*, if you haven’t already. Until the refill goes through, you will need to borrow from the Poeppel fund – Jeff will generally take care of this, or ask lab members to front the cash for a few days, and you'll pay them back once refilled. If you do the latter, be sure to hold those interim receipts out of the refill, as they'll be counted in the next refill. 

If you find that the lab is consistently running through cash faster than you can refill it, you can ask Roman  about increasing our petty cash limit.

### Behavioral Experiment Techniques

### MTurk
We design and run behavioral experiments using **[Amazon's Mechanical Turk](https://www.mturk.com/mturk/welcome)**, a crowdsourcing Internet marketplace. Here's some important AMT lingo:

- <i>HIT:</i> the actual survey/experiment that is posted
- <i>requester:</i> us, requesting participants to complete our HITs
- <i>worker:</i> subjects

We use **[turktools](http://turktools.net/)**, an open-source site containing tools for creating linguistic surveys on MTurk, to get HITs up and running. The site includes skeleton .html and .py files to help you create a csv file with HITs and fields that you will eventually upload.

### Ibex Farm
We use **[Ibex Farm](http://spellout.net/ibexfarm/login)** for experiments requiring precise timing information such as reaction times. Since we can't rely on the timing of each individual Turker's computer, the experiment is hosted on a server (the "Farm"). Ibex experiments are written in JavaScript, but, luckily, you don’t have to know much JavaScript to use Ibex. For the most part, you can stick to the templates and examples provided on our Wiki and on the Ibex server.

### Prolific
Most people build their behavioral experiments on Ibex Farm and mount them on **[Prolific](https://app.prolific.co/)**, where participants are paid through the site to complete the study. For this, you can make your own account. To mount an Ibex experiment on Prolific, under the 'Data Collection' section of a new study, select 'External study link' and provide the link to your published Ibex study in the URL field. Fill out the rest of the information according to your study, and adjust the payment to come as close to $15USD/hr as you can. Then, to load money into Prolific, navigate to the 'Projects' tab on the left and select your study. On the far right, there is a dropdown menu called 'Action'. Click this and select request a quote. Send this quote to Teresa Leung to process the transaction, and sooner or later your account will have sufficient funds to run your study.

### Concur

Nirvana now operates using Concur, an online booking system, for *staff and faculty*. You can find it through the **[NYUAD Intranet](https://intranet.nyuad.nyu.edu/)**. Alec and Liina, as well as all staff and post-docs, have Concur accounts. When booking travel for these members, you'll need to "act as" another user by clicking `Profile > Acting as other user` and choosing a user (see how to gain access to their accounts below)

On Concur, create segments for different travel requests: flight, accommodations, and, if traveling to NYUAD, limousine service. We currently don't provide transport to and from the airport *on the way* to Abu Dhabi or when folks are traveling to other places for conferences, etc.. If the trip is to NYUAD, they will most likely ask you to confirm ground transport (each way is AED110). The amount that you request on Concur (in AED) can be a rough estimate, as Nirvana will send quotes that you'll then choose from.

### Acting as Other Users

In order to submit requests for anyone with a Concur account (typically for Alec or Liina), you'll need to request to be a "Travel Assistant/Delegate." Email `nyuad.it@nyu.edu` with the NetIDs of the faculty/staff you're wanting to book for. Additionally, forward them an email from each user stating they approve of you as their travel assistant. They'll then set you up as a Travel/Expense delegate for each user. This is sort of a pain, but it only needs to happen once!

### Student Travel

To request travel for PhD students, you must fill out an NYUAD Travel and Accommodation Request Form (located on the server) and send it to Nirvana. The form includes flight and accommodation information, chartfield and approver signatures, and secure passenger data (i.e. Passport information) for those traveling for the first time on behalf of NYUAD. Nirvana will then send quotes for the items you requested, and you can confer with the student about which options they choose.

We provide flight and accommodations for those presenting at conferences. You'll often have to bug students about their travel, as they'll likely not remember to ask you about it until the last minute.

### Travel Exceptions

Students will often want to tack on a leg to their travel to go home over the summer, or if they'll be flying from a location other than NYC or AD. The easiest way to do this is to have Nirvana book all flights and have the student pay the difference between the entire trip and just a roundtrip to AD (or wherever the conference, for example, may be). In this case, you'll want to apply for a travel exception by emailing `	nyuad.travelexception@nyu.edu` with the reason for the exception, as well as email approval from Alec. If for some reason the student needs to book urgently, they can purchase on their own and be reimbursed. However, the reimbursement process is quite ugly so, if you can, stick to having them pay the difference.

### Time Off

*Note: this section only applies to full time, non-student employees, but I'm leaving it in in the case that the lab hires a full time, non-student employee for after you graduate*

Employees accrue 22 vacation days per academic year at a rate of 1.83 days per month. We also have two personal days as well as sick time. To access your available time off, go to the `Work` tab at **[NYUHome](https://globalhome.nyu.edu/group/nyu-home/work)** and select PeopleSync > Absence. Alec and Liina typically approve every request automatically, but be sure to run dates by them via Slack before requesting them on PeopleSync to make sure they know. Your time off resets to 11 days (77 hours) at the beginning of the next academic year (September 1), so be sure to use your days! *You will get paid for unused vacation days upon termination of your position.*

Technically, new employees have a 6-month "probationary period" in which you can't request time off. Since RAs/lab managers start in the summer and will most likely take vacation, you can get verbal permission from Liina and Alec and then request later dates on PeopleSync once the probation period is over.

In the event that you have to come in on a weekend or holiday to run participants, you should take a day off to make up for it.

### Hiring

When it is time to hire a new staff member, you may be responsible for drafting an ad (previous versions live here: NEUROLING > Lab Manager > Hiring/Open House > Pre/post-doc ads). Before distributing the ad, contact Ranee (`ranee.patel@nyu.edu`; non-AD hires) or Dede ( `deirdre.tabak@nyu.edu`; AD hires) and ask them to post the job description on the appropriate portal. You will then be asked to distribute the ad via various newsletters and listservs. The nyuneurolinguisticslab gmail account is a good one from which to send listserv announcements if you want to maintain anonymity.

### Offboarding

When preparing to leave your position, contact your HR rep (Ranee if non-AD, Dede if AD hire) to initiate this process at least 4 weeks before your desired end date. Please do your best to update the guides, organize your files, gather any other training materials, etc. before you leave.
